DIRECTION 1: Label-Free Pre-infection Morphology Predicts HCMV Infection Speed
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

THE CORE FINDING
A single brightfield snapshot taken ~10 frames BEFORE any fluorescent reporter
is visible predicts whether a cell will progress to late gene expression fast or
slow (AUC = 0.876, BrightFieldEmbedding/07_bf_b2r_analysis.py). This beats:
  - GFP at onset (AUC = 0.742)
  - Tabular features at onset (AUC ~ 0.685)
  - Any GFP+BF fusion tested (best: 0.712)

WHY IT'S NOVEL
No published paper reports deep morphological embeddings (Cellpose SAM) from
brightfield alone as predictors of individual HCMV kinetics (continuous delay,
not just who gets infected). Existing work uses coarse features (cell size, cell
cycle phase from fluorescent reporters, manual scoring). This is a label-free,
pre-infection signal with genuinely high predictive power.

OPEN BIOLOGICAL QUESTION
What in the cell's morphology is predictive? The Cellpose embedding is 256-dim
and encodes nuclear shape, size, texture, etc. The literature implicates cell
cycle phase as a source of infection heterogeneity. The key question: is BF at
onset-10 just a proxy for cell cycle, or does it encode additional information?

NEXT ANALYSES
1. Interpret the embedding: UMAP on the 256-dim BF embeddings colored by b2r
   delay. Which of the top-20 predictive dims correlate with interpretable
   quantities (cell area, nuclear circularity, BF texture)?
2. Decompose vs. cell cycle: Add FUCCI cell cycle reporter (or use nuclear area
   as a proxy — G2/M cells are larger). Test whether BF embedding predicts speed
   over and above cell cycle phase alone (partial correlation). If yes, this is
   a genuinely new biological predictor beyond cell cycle.
3. Attention on embedding dimensions: Use grad-CAM or saliency maps on the
   Cellpose encoder to identify which spatial regions of the cell at onset-10
   drive the prediction (nucleus? cytoplasm?). The Attention_maps/ directory
   already exists in BrightFieldEmbedding/.
4. Test at earlier timepoints: Does AUC hold at onset-20 or onset-30? Is the
   predictive signal stable over time, or does it accumulate as the virus
   approaches? This argues for/against a static cell state vs. a slowly building
   host response. The more_time_test/ module is relevant here.
5. Perturbation experiment: Treat cells with latrunculin A (cytoskeletal
   disruption) or nocodazole (mitotic arrest) before infection. If the BF
   predictor fails, this implicates cytoskeletal/nuclear organization as the
   biological basis.

DIRECTION 2: Non-productive HCMV Infection — Survival Analysis & Predictability
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

THE FINDING
~38% of GFP+ cells never turn mCherry+ during the movie. The KM analysis
(future_infection/future_infection.py) shows the KM cure fraction is ~30%, and
log-normal extrapolation suggests most non-productive cells are truly censored
rather than permanently abortive. However, ~30% may represent a real cure
fraction (cells that will never complete infection).

WHY IT'S NOVEL
No published HCMV live-imaging study has performed formal single-cell survival
analysis properly accounting for individual censoring windows. Most papers
report the fraction non-productive at movie end (naive counting). The
methodology here — KM + parametric fitting with individual observation windows
— is the rigorous approach and reaches a quantitatively different conclusion.

OPEN BIOLOGICAL QUESTION
Are non-productive cells a distinct pre-existing subpopulation, or are they
stochastically sampled from the same distribution?

NEXT ANALYSES
1. Can BF at onset-10 discriminate productive from non-productive? Train the
   same LogReg/Cellpose embedding pipeline with productive (label=1) vs.
   non-productive (label=0) as target. The InfectedvsUninfected/ module already
   predicts who gets infected — this asks whether you can predict who will
   COMPLETE infection among already-GFP+ cells. Biologically deeper question.
2. UMAP of BF embedding colored by productive status: do productive and
   non-productive cells overlap, or does non-productive have a distinct
   morphological niche?
3. Competing-risks framing: Model infection speed (g2r) and productive outcome
   jointly as competing risks — fast-progressors have lower censoring probability
   than slow-progressors; this could bias the b2r cutoff if not accounted for.
4. Experiment — ISG reporter: Non-productive cells often show strong
   interferon-stimulated gene (ISG) expression. Add an ISG reporter (e.g.,
   MxA-GFP) as a fourth channel. Test if non-productive cells are in a
   pre-existing antiviral state that is visible in the BF embedding.

DIRECTION 3: Single-Cell Spatial Epidemiology of HCMV Infection Waves
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

THE FINDING
proximity_analysis/proximity_analysis.py: n_infected_at_onset correlates with
g2r delay (Spearman rho = -0.317, p < 0.001), but after partialling out onset
timing the effect disappears (partial rho = -0.032, p = 0.46). This means
early-onset cells have fewer infected neighbors — they are the initiators,
physically isolated. Late-onset cells are near many already-infected cells —
they were likely infected by cell-to-cell spread.

WHY IT'S NOVEL
Spatial autocorrelation of HCMV infection KINETICS (not just spread probability)
has not been published. The literature confirms cell-to-cell spread produces
radial infection foci, but whether cells infected by free virus vs. cell-to-cell
spread have different g2r kinetics is unresolved.

OPEN BIOLOGICAL QUESTION
Does the mode of viral entry (free virus vs. cell-to-cell spread) determine
infection speed?

NEXT ANALYSES
1. Moran's I for onset timing: Test for spatial autocorrelation of GFP onset
   time across the well. Spatial clustering argues for a seeding-then-spread
   model.
2. Onset wave mapping: Visualize infection spread as a spatiotemporal movie —
   cells colored by onset frame. Identify primary infection foci (early, no
   infected neighbors) vs. secondary (late, surrounded by prior-infected cells).
   Compare their b2r delay distributions directly.
3. Mode-of-entry stratification: Cells that turned GFP+ early and in isolation
   are candidate free-virus infections; cells that turned late with many
   GFP+ neighbors are candidate cell-to-cell. Test whether b2r distributions
   differ between these groups.
4. Experiment — neutralizing antibody block: Add anti-HCMV neutralizing antibody
   to block free-virus spread. All infections should then be cell-to-cell.
   Compare g2r distributions vs. unblocked control. If cell-to-cell infected
   cells have faster/slower g2r, this resolves the mechanism.

DIRECTION 4: GFP Kinetics as an Early Predictor of Late Gene Expression Timing
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

THE FINDING
The Forecast/ module builds a TabICL trajectory predictor using 9 features x
up to 275 frames. The mCherry trajectory can be predicted from prior context
windows. The tensor is (497, 275, 9): GFP, Nuc_BFP, BF_mean, BF_contrast,
Cell_area, Nuc_area, Speed, Circ_cell, Circ_nuc.

WHY IT'S NOVEL
Predicting HCMV late gene expression from the SHAPE of early IE (GFP) kinetics,
rather than from a single snapshot, has not been systematically explored. The
field knows IE levels matter (IE1/IE2 ratio), but the quantitative relationship
between IE ramp-up speed and late onset at the single-cell level is unknown.

OPEN BIOLOGICAL QUESTION
Does the slope and peak of GFP accumulation in the first hours post-onset
predict when mCherry will appear?

NEXT ANALYSES
1. GFP ramp features: Extract per-cell statistics from the GFP trajectory in
   the first N frames post-onset: slope, AUC, time to half-max, peak value.
   Correlate with g2r delay. More interpretable than the TabICL black box.
2. Earliest predictive window: At what point (how many frames post-GFP onset)
   does prediction of mCherry onset become better than chance? This tells you
   how early the fate decision is encoded in the dynamics.
3. GFP kinetics vs. BF morphology — head-to-head: does adding GFP trajectory
   features to BF embedding at onset-10 improve AUC? GFP at onset (single
   frame) doesn't beat BF alone at onset-10, but GFP TRAJECTORY may be
   different.
4. Experiment — IE2-specific reporter: IE2 (UL122) is the key transactivator
   of late gene expression. A cell line with IE2-mScarlet and a late gene-GFP
   would let you test if IE2 ramp-up specifically (not just total IE1/IE2) is
   the predictive signal.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
DIRECTION 5: Cross-Condition Transfer and Biological Generalizability
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

THE FINDING
B3 transfer experiments (scripts/12_xgboost_transfer_B3.py,
scripts/22_xgboost_transfer_B3_full.py) test whether models trained on A2/A3
generalize to B3. Notable: BF alone on A3 gives AUC=0.783 vs. A2's 0.686,
suggesting the conditions differ meaningfully.

WHY IT MATTERS
If BF morphological predictors are universal (not condition-specific), this is
a major result — the same cell-intrinsic state that predicts speed in one
experiment predicts it in another. If they fail to transfer, it points to
condition-specific biology (e.g., MOI, cell passage, seeding time).

NEXT ANALYSES
1. Characterize A2 vs. A3 vs. B3 differences: Are these different cell
   densities, MOIs, time post-seeding? Why does A3 BF alone outperform A2 BF?
2. Latent space alignment: Project A2 and A3 BF embeddings into the same
   UMAP/PCA space. Do fast cells from A2 and A3 cluster together? This tests
   whether the predictive signal is the same biological phenomenon.
3. Systematic condition variation experiment: Image cells at different time
   points post-seeding (day 1, 2, 3 before infection) and test BF predictability.
   If AUC rises with post-seeding time, cells are settling into a morphological
   state that predicts fate.

PART II — EXPLORATION & CHARACTERIZATION OF SINGLE-CELL INFECTION DYNAMICS
═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Directions 1–5 are predictive (use the pre-infection state to forecast fate).
Directions 6–9 below are descriptive: instead of asking "what predicts speed?"
they ask "what actually happens to a cell, frame by frame, as HCMV takes it
over?" These exploit per-frame TrackMate features that are currently used only
as model inputs, never characterized in their own right. The raw tracks
(CompleteImage/*_Merged_spots.csv, *_BrightField_spots.csv) carry, for every
cell at every 15-min frame: X, Y, Area, Circ., Solidity, Shape index, elliptic
long/short axis, and TrackMate motility-mode probabilities (P stuck,
P diffusive). The Forecast tensor (497×275×9, Forecast/tensor_X.npz) already
aligns 9 of these per cell. Pixel scale 0.2871 µm/px; ~68 h per movie.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
DIRECTION 6: The Single-Cell Morphodynamic Signature of HCMV Infection
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

THE OPPORTUNITY
We have the full morphometric time course of every cell across ~68 h at 15-min
resolution, with three internal clocks per cell (green/blue/red onsets). The
existing onset-aligned analyses (Brightfield_analysis/04_onset_aligned_speed.py,
05_onset_aligned_size.py) align only single features to GFP onset. The
opportunity is a unified, multi-feature "morphodynamic signature": the average
trajectory of area, circularity, solidity, elongation, and speed, aligned not
just to GFP onset but to each gene-expression milestone (green, blue, red),
with a proper pre-infection baseline.

WHY IT'S NOVEL
HCMV cytopathic effect (cell rounding, enlargement, eventual detachment) is
classically described qualitatively, by eye, at fixed endpoints — e.g. rounding
and shrinkage first seen ~72 h post-infection by phase-contrast/EM (PubMed
PMID 16546736). No published HCMV study resolves the *continuous timing* of each
morphological change and locks it to the molecular program (IE→early→late) in
the same cell. Converting "CPE appears around 3 days" into "circularity inflects
X hours after IE onset and Y hours before late-gene onset" is a genuinely new,
quantitative phenotyping of the cytopathic program.

OPEN BIOLOGICAL QUESTION
In what order, and with what lag relative to the gene-expression cascade, do the
morphological hallmarks of infection appear — and is that ordering fixed across
cells or does it vary with infection speed?

NEXT ANALYSES
1. Milestone-aligned multi-feature panel: For each feature (area, Circ.,
   Solidity, Shape index, aspect ratio = El.long/El.short, speed), plot the
   population-mean trajectory ± CI aligned separately to green, blue, and red
   onset. Use the pre-GFP frames as the within-cell baseline (z-score each cell
   to its own first ~10 frames) so changes are intrinsic, not absolute-scale.
2. Change-point timing: Per cell, detect the inflection frame of each feature
   (e.g. ruptures/PELT change-point detection). Report the distribution of
   "frames from GFP onset to rounding onset," etc. This is the quantitative
   replacement for "CPE at 72 h."
3. Order-of-events map: Within each cell, rank the milestones (IE onset, area
   inflection, rounding inflection, motility drop, late-gene onset) on the
   shared clock. Is the sequence canonical (a fixed cascade) or shuffled?
4. Speed-stratified morphodynamics: Split by g2r tertile (early/medium/late;
   already defined in 02_build_3d_table.py) and overlay the signatures. Do
   fast cells just run the same program compressed in time, or a different
   program? Time-warping (DTW) alignment tests "same shape, different rate."
5. Decouple from the cell-cycle prior: enlargement could be G2/M, not CPE. Use
   nuclear-area baseline as a cycle proxy and confirm the post-onset trajectory
   departs from any pre-onset cycle-driven drift.

EXPERIMENT
Phase/BF-only time lapse with a single IE-mScarlet reporter and a cell-cycle
(FUCCI) reporter, no perturbation — a clean dataset to time the cytopathic
program against both the viral clock and the cell-cycle clock simultaneously.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
DIRECTION 7: Cytomegaly Kinetics — Cell and Nuclear Enlargement in Real Time
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

THE OPPORTUNITY
"Cytomegalovirus" is literally named for the giant-cell phenotype it induces.
We have per-frame Cell_area and Nuc_area for every cell (tensor dims 4 and 5;
raw Area in *_Merged_spots.csv and *_nuclei_spots.csv). This lets us turn the
defining phenotype of the virus into a measured single-cell growth curve.

WHY IT'S NOVEL
Cytomegaly is universally cited but, to our knowledge, never quantified as a
continuous single-cell enlargement *rate* time-locked to gene expression.
Whether the nucleus enlarges before, with, or after the cytoplasm — and whether
the nucleus-to-cell area ratio rises (consistent with viral replication
compartment expansion filling the nucleus) — is unresolved at single-cell,
live resolution.

OPEN BIOLOGICAL QUESTION
Is cytomegaly a smooth post-IE swelling, or step-like at the early→late
transition? Does the magnitude or rate of enlargement correlate with how fast
(g2r) or whether (productive vs. non-productive) a cell completes infection?

NEXT ANALYSES
1. Single-cell growth curves: area(t) per cell, baseline-normalized, aligned to
   each onset. Fit per-cell enlargement rate (µm²/h) and onset-of-enlargement.
   Report fold-change from baseline to red onset; convert px² to µm² (×0.2871²).
2. Nuclear vs. cytoplasmic enlargement: track Nuc_area/Cell_area ratio over
   time. A rising ratio late = nucleus-dominated swelling (replication
   compartment); flat ratio = whole-cell swelling.
3. Couple to motility & rounding: enlargement should co-occur with rounding
   (↑circularity) and a motility drop (Direction 8) — test the cross-feature
   timing (does swelling precede or follow rounding?).
4. Enlargement vs. fate: does enlargement rate differ between fast/slow (g2r)
   and between productive and non-productive (Direction 2) cells? Non-productive
   GFP+ cells that never enlarge would be a striking abortive phenotype.
5. Cross-condition: compare cytomegaly kinetics across A2/A3/B3 — does a
   condition with faster g2r also enlarge faster (Direction 5 link)?

EXPERIMENT
Membrane + NLS-tagged dual reporter (e.g. mScarlet-CAAX + BFP-NLS) for precise
cytoplasmic and nuclear volume segmentation; add a viral DNA / replication-
compartment marker (e.g. UL44-GFP) to test directly whether nuclear enlargement
tracks replication-compartment growth.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
DIRECTION 8: Motility Reprogramming of Infected Fibroblasts
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

THE OPPORTUNITY
Speed (px/frame) is already computed per cell per frame (01_compute_speed.py),
and the raw tracks additionally carry TrackMate motility-mode probabilities
(P stuck, P diffusive) and X,Y for directional analysis. Because the dataset
includes uninfected/bystander tracks and non-productive GFP+ cells, we can
contrast motility across infection status AND across infection stage within
the same well.

WHY IT'S NOVEL
The HCMV motility literature is almost entirely about the viral chemokine
receptor US28 driving *smooth-muscle-cell* migration (PubMed PMID 14506272,
DOI 10.1074/jbc.M307936200; PMID 23303826, DOI 10.1099/vir.0.047290-0), and the
MCMV homolog M33 explicitly induces SMC but NOT fibroblast migration (PMID
16051870, DOI 10.1128/JVI.79.16.10788-10795.2005). So what HCMV does to
*fibroblast* motility — the cell type imaged here — is essentially
uncharacterized, and is the dominant model substrate for HCMV. A quantitative,
stage-resolved motility trajectory in fibroblasts is new.

OPEN BIOLOGICAL QUESTION
Do infected fibroblasts speed up early (an active, virus-driven motile phase)
and then arrest as cytomegaly/rounding sets in — and is the motility change a
cause or merely a consequence of the morphological program?

NEXT ANALYSES
1. Speed trajectory by status & stage: mean speed aligned to GFP onset for
   (a) uninfected, (b) productive-fast, (c) productive-slow, (d) non-productive
   GFP+ cells. Extends 04_onset_aligned_speed.py to a status×stage grid.
2. Motility-mode transitions: use P stuck / P diffusive to classify each
   cell-frame as stuck/diffusive/directed; build the fraction-in-each-mode over
   the infection clock. Does infection drive a diffusive→stuck transition, and
   when relative to red onset?
3. Directional persistence & MSD: from X,Y, compute per-window mean-squared
   displacement and a persistence ratio (net/total path). Test whether early
   infection transiently increases directed migration (the fibroblast analog of
   the US28/SMC phenotype) before arrest.
4. Motility vs. enlargement coupling: regress instantaneous speed on
   instantaneous area within cell — is the arrest explained by size alone, or
   independent?
5. Spatial coupling (link to Direction 3): do cells that move more before onset
   contribute to spreading infection to neighbors (track displacement vs.
   becoming a future infection focus)?

EXPERIMENT
US28-deletion (ΔUS28) vs. wild-type HCMV, same imaging pipeline: test whether
any infected-fibroblast motility change is US28-dependent (as in SMC) or a
distinct fibroblast program. Optionally a scratch/wound-migration assay under
infection to amplify directional signal.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
DIRECTION 9: An Unsupervised Atlas of Single-Cell Infection Trajectories
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

THE OPPORTUNITY
Directions 6–8 characterize features one at a time. Direction 9 integrates them:
treat each cell as a multivariate trajectory (the 497×275×9 tensor) and learn,
without using fate labels, the natural archetypes of infection progression and
the transitions between cell states. This reframes the project from "predict a
scalar delay" to "map the landscape of infection trajectories."

WHY IT'S NOVEL
Morphological profiling and morphodynamic trajectory clustering are established
in cell biology (e.g. deep morphology embeddings, Nature Methods 2024,
s41592-024-02352-2, already cited below) but have not been applied to build an
unsupervised state atlas of HCMV infection from live single-cell tracks. It also
provides an interpretable counterpart to the black-box BF-embedding predictor
(Direction 1): the predictive embedding lives at one timepoint; this is the
whole movie.

OPEN BIOLOGICAL QUESTION
Are there discrete infection "routes" (e.g. enlarge-then-arrest vs.
arrest-then-enlarge vs. abortive) or one continuum that cells traverse at
different speeds? Do non-productive cells occupy a distinct branch?

NEXT ANALYSES
1. Trajectory clustering: time-warp-align (DTW) the per-cell 9-feature
   trajectories and cluster (k-means on DTW, or a trajectory VAE / time-series
   transformer latent). Characterize each cluster's morphodynamic profile.
2. State-space embedding: PHATE/UMAP on per-frame feature vectors pooled across
   cells, then overlay each cell's path as a curve through the state space —
   visualize infection as flow on a manifold; mark green/blue/red onset points.
3. Pseudotime: fit a pseudotime to the manifold and test how well it recovers
   the true molecular clock (correlate pseudotime with g2r) — a morphology-only
   ordering of infection progress.
4. Branch vs. fate: relate clusters/branches to productive vs. non-productive
   (Direction 2), g2r tertile, and condition (A2/A3/B3). Do morphodynamic
   archetypes carry biological meaning the scalar delay misses?
5. Tie back to prediction: do cells from different archetypes have different
   Direction-1 BF-embedding signatures at onset-10? Links the descriptive atlas
   to the predictive result.

EXPERIMENT
Higher-temporal-resolution imaging (e.g. 5-min frames) on a subset to test
whether fast transitions seen as single steps at 15 min resolve into finer
substructure — validates that the archetypes are not an artifact of sampling
rate.

The strongest standalone paper combines Directions 1 and 2:

  "Pre-infection cell morphology predicts HCMV infection kinetics and
   productive outcome at the single-cell level"

Narrative arc:
  1. Single HCMV-infected cells are highly heterogeneous in infection speed —
     quantify rigorously with KM survival analysis (Direction 2, methodological
     contribution).
  2. This heterogeneity is partially encoded in the cell's morphological state
     BEFORE infection is visible — show BF AUC = 0.876 (Direction 1, main
     result).
  3. The signal is label-free, outperforms handcrafted tabular features, and
     is captured by deep morphological embeddings.
  4. Predictive embedding dimensions point toward [cell cycle / nuclear
     organization / cytoskeletal state] as the biological basis (new experiments
     in Direction 1).

KEY EXPERIMENT NEEDED BEFORE SUBMISSION:
  FUCCI cell cycle reporter — because cell cycle is the existing prior in the
  literature, you need to either confirm it explains the BF signal or show BF
  adds information beyond cell cycle alone.

A SECOND, COMPLEMENTARY PAPER combines Directions 6–9 (descriptive, not
predictive):

  "Real-time morphodynamic phenotyping of the HCMV cytopathic program at
   single-cell resolution"

Narrative arc:
  1. The HCMV cytopathic effect — cytomegaly, rounding, motility arrest — has
     only ever been described qualitatively at fixed endpoints. We measure it
     continuously, per cell, time-locked to the IE→early→late molecular clock
     (Directions 6–7).
  2. Quantify when each hallmark appears relative to gene expression, and show
     the program runs faster (not differently) in fast-progressing cells.
  3. Fibroblast motility reprogramming is characterized for the first time, in
     contrast to the SMC/US28-centric literature (Direction 8).
  4. Integrate into an unsupervised trajectory atlas that distinguishes
     productive routes from abortive ones (Direction 9), giving an interpretable
     companion to the predictive embedding paper above.

This pairs naturally with the predictive paper: paper 1 says the fate is
pre-encoded; paper 2 shows the fate playing out. Together they argue HCMV
infection speed is a cell-intrinsic, morphologically legible program.
